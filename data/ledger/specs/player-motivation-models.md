## The models, in order

- **Bartle, 1996** ("Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players who suit MUDs"): four types on two axes (acting on / interacting
  with, the world / players): Achievers, Explorers, Socializers, Killers. Drawn from MUD players, not measured.
- **Bartle, 2003** (Designing Virtual Worlds): a third axis, implicit / explicit, and eight types: Opportunist and Planner
  (achievers), Hacker and Scientist (explorers), Friend and Networker (socializers), Griefer and Politician (killers).
- **Nick Yee, 2006**: a factor analysis of questions built on Bartle's types found three components with ten sub-motivations:
  Achievement (advancement, mechanics, competition), Social (socializing, relationship, teamwork), Immersion (discovery,
  role-playing, customization, escapism). Its key finding: motivations are scales that coexist in one player, not types that
  exclude each other.
- **BrainHex, 2011** (Nacke, Bateman, Mandryk; from neurobiology and Bateman's earlier demographic models, 50,000+ players):
  seven archetypes: Seeker, Survivor, Daredevil, Mastermind, Conqueror, Socialiser, Achiever.
- **Marczewski's HEXAD** (for gamification): Philanthropist, Socialiser, Free Spirit, Achiever, Disruptor, Player; tied to
  intrinsic motivations (relatedness, autonomy, mastery, purpose) and extrinsic rewards.
- **Quantic Foundry's Gamer Motivation Model** (Yee and Ducheneaut, from 2015; hundreds of thousands of players): twelve motivations
  in six pairs, and three high-level clusters:
  - Action: Destruction, Excitement
  - Social: Competition, Community
  - Mastery: Challenge, Strategy
  - Achievement: Completion, Power
  - Immersion: Fantasy, Story
  - Creativity: Design, Discovery
  - clusters: Action-Social, Mastery-Achievement, Immersion-Creativity (Discovery bridges Immersion-Creativity and
    Mastery-Achievement, Power bridges Action-Social and Mastery-Achievement).
- **A 2024 meta-ethnography** (McKechnie-Martin et al., Games and Culture): "The 28 Dimensions of Play", a synthesis of the player
  motivation studies.

## What it means for the Adventure Journal (proposed)

Bartle's four still work as a shorthand, but the measured models say a player is a mix, and on a solo server the Killer and
Socializer halves are thin. Quantic Foundry's six pairs fit a solo classic journal better: Completion (collections, explore every
zone), Power (levels, gear, the raids), Challenge and Strategy (hard fights, feats), Discovery (exploration, hidden things, lore
books), Story and Fantasy (questlines, lore, the world's people), Community and Competition (world events, battlegrounds, honor),
Destruction and Excitement (the few chaotic ones). A challenge can carry two motivations, and the Journal can later weigh which ones
a character leans to (what they complete most) to suggest the next challenges, without calling the player anything.

Culture).
